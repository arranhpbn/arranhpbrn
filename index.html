the name
arran
/ ˈærən / — two syllables, stress on first
Origin
Scottish Gaelic — from the Isle of Arran, off Scotland's west coast
Root meaning
From àrainn — possibly "kidney-shaped", describing the island's outline from above
Alternate theory
Linked to old Irish ara — used to describe high, arched land
Element
Associated with earth and sea — the island sits between Scotland and Ireland
The Isle of Arran has been inhabited for over 6,000 years. Sometimes called "Scotland in miniature", its dramatic landscape ranges from lowland farms to highland peaks — a fitting origin for a name that carries both weight and warmth.
Notable Arrans
Isle of Arran, Scotland Arran Johnston (musician) Arran Henderson (rugby)
the name
HEPBURN
/ ˈhɛpbɜːrn / — two syllables, stress on first
Origin
Old English — a place-name turned surname, from Northumberland
Literal meaning
heope (wild rose hip) + burn (stream) = "stream by the rose hips"
Burn
Still used today — burn means a small stream, from Old English burna
First recorded
Appears in records as early as the 14th century in northern England
Hepburn is a topographic surname — given to families who lived near a landmark, here a stream lined with wild rose bushes. It speaks to a time when identity was rooted in the landscape around you.
Notable Hepburns
Audrey Hepburn Katharine Hepburn James Hepburn, Earl of Bothwell
